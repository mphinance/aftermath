LDI write-up: loanDepot (LDI) is an explicit bet on the rate cycle. We were up more than 150% on the stock at one point and are now slightly in the red, largely because the rate backdrop has moved against the thesis. The setup remains simple: mortgage originators are deeply out of favor, volumes are suppressed, and the companies that survive the trough with their infrastructure intact should have major operating leverage when the refinance cycle eventually turns. The key asset is loanDepot’s servicing platform, where its organic refinance consumer direct recapture rate increased to 73% in Q1 from 71% in Q4. In plain English, that means when borrowers already in loanDepot’s servicing book refinance, loanDepot is keeping a very high share of that business instead of losing those customers to competitors. That matters because customer acquisition costs are high in mortgage, and having the borrower relationship before the refi wave begins is a real advantage. The broader industry setup also remains interesting, as banks have pulled back from parts of mortgage lending while nonbank lenders have taken share, which should matter if refinance activity returns. The problem is that the macro has become less cooperative, with BofA now calling for three Fed hikes this year. We are long LDI as a rate-cycle call option, with the view that a future refinance wave could unlock significant operating leverage if rates finally move in the right direction.

As quantum moves from research toward deployment and security migration, companies building trusted infrastructure for governments, enterprises, and financial systems should become increasingly important. Our bet is on vertically integrated BTQ, which remains a high-upside, speculative position.

IPWR write-up: Ideal Power (IPWR) is a pre-revenue semiconductor company, but the setup is interesting. The company has a sub-$100 million enterprise value, roughly $46 million in cash after two recent financings, and a deep patent portfolio around its B-TRAN technology. In simple terms, B-TRAN is a bidirectional power switch, meaning it can move electricity efficiently in both directions using one device instead of requiring multiple conventional switches. That matters for applications like EVs, battery storage, grid infrastructure, and AI data centers, where power needs to move quickly and efficiently between on-site generation, batteries, and the utility grid. The company already has an active Stellantis purchase order, a multi-year cooperation agreement with Lazzen targeting solid-state circuit breakers for AI data centers, and an LOI with a leading Asian power module manufacturer. The dilution from its recent raises was painful, but the result is a much stronger balance sheet, with enough cash to fund several years of development at the current burn rate. Automotive qualification is expected to complete in summer 2026, which gives the story a clear near-term catalyst. This remains a high-risk, high-reward semiconductor bet, but at today’s enterprise value, we think the market is giving very little credit for the potential of a differentiated power-switching technology sitting directly in front of major electrification and AI infrastructure trends.
