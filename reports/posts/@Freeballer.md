**September Is Here**



And every year the same shit happens

People look at the calendar, see September, see “historically the worst month,” see red candles, and immediately start screaming:

“THE MARKET IS FUCKED.”

Relax

September is historically ugly. Since 1928, the S&P 500 has averaged roughly a -1.1% to -1.2% September return, with September producing more down months than any other month

The Nasdaq has historically averaged around -0.8%. (The Wall Street Journal⁠￼)

But here’s the part people conveniently forget

Historical weakness does NOT mean automatic collapse

The market can absolutely rip higher in September

And 2026 has another variable sitting directly on top of all of this 


TRUMP + THE MIDTERMS

We’re entering a period where policy, tariffs, taxes, regulation, interest rates, government spending, geopolitical headlines and election positioning can all move markets

And the market doesn’t give a shit about your political opinion

It cares about earnings, liquidity, rates, inflation, positioning and expectations

The 2026 midterms make the next few months especially interesting

Historically, midterm years can be choppy heading into Election Day, but the historical pattern AFTER the election is a completely different story

Since 1974, the S&P 500 has averaged roughly +5.7% in the three months following midterm elections, with 11 of 13 periods positive

Six months after the midterms?

+12.4% average


All 13 historical periods were positive.
(Schwab Brokerage⁠￼)

That’s why I’m not interested in the

“September = crash”

narrative.

I’m interested in what the market actually does.

Watch SPX

Watch volume

Watch breadth

Watch whether dips are being bought or sold

Watch whether major support levels are defended

Watch whether new highs are being confirmed by participation or whether the index is being held up by a handful of mega caps

Because here’s the thing

PRICE TELLS YOU WHAT IS HAPPENING.
VOLUME TELLS YOU HOW MUCH PARTICIPATION IS BEHIND IT

Low-volume weakness isn’t necessarily the same thing as institutional distribution

And lower volume strength isn’t necessarily the same thing as a healthy breakout

You want to see confirmation!!!!

If SPX breaks support with expanding volume, breadth deteriorates and volatility starts expanding?

Pay attention to that area right there. 

If SPX sells off, volume stays relatively contained, sellers fail to follow through and buyers step in at important levels?

That’s a completely different fucking market.

And don’t forget the other side of September

October exists

Then November

Then December

Historically, November through April has been the strongest six-month stretch for stocks. (Fidelity⁠)

So a September pullback isn’t automatically the beginning of the end

Sometimes it’s simply the market resetting positioning before the next leg
