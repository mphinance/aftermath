Think of CPI (Consumer Price Index) like checking the thermostat in your house.

🌡️ Too Hot (High CPI / Higher Inflation)
Prices are rising faster than expected.
The Fed may keep interest rates higher—or even raise them—just to cool things down.

➡️ That can be bad for stocks, especially growth and tech names, because borrowing money becomes more expensive. Then the monthly payment is horrible.

❄️ Too Cold (Low CPI / Cooling Inflation)
Prices aren’t rising as fast.
The Fed has less pressure to raise rates and may even consider cutting them in the future. Booyah!!

➡️ That is often good for stocks, because lower interest rates generally help businesses and investors.
