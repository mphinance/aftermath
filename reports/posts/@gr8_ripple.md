**The Architect**

Let’s be real, at the core of our strategies, we are all investors. The long term investor is a person that is of the mindset, “Time in the market beats timing the market” It’s the “less sexy” strategy because social media peeps can’t make content about it. “Buy $SPY and hold it for 20 years.” Great advice, but not great for the content creator purposes.

The long term investor’s motto, “Time builds wealth”. Through the ups and the downs, the long term investor cannot be shaken. Long term investors are typically the people who have unshakeable conviction in a company and thesis for the long term. Huge drawdowns are a perfect buying opportunity for them and they love to DCA. Take my uncle for example, he has been buying McDonalds stock every month for the last 20 years. Needless to say, he isn’t going hungry. Every time I try to talk to him about investing, all he says is, “DCA, DCA, DCA”. Hey, works for him, he has figured out his system. When I try to talk to him about tickers I am interested in or get wind about, he couldn’t care less. He has the companies he knows and believes in and to him, that’s all he needs.

Stocks that this strategy is typically interested in are companies with a wide economic moat that produce consistent cash flow. They typically invest in ETF’s, the MAG-7, and dividend paying stocks. They also pick companies that tend to be “recession resistant” such as Walmart, Pepsi, Costco etc. 

The ultimate edge in this strategy is patience. Do you have the disciple to set it and forget it and let time do its thing?✌️

**DD template**

I saw someone ask for a template in order to conduct their own DD. For many lurkers on her like myself, it might seem overwhelming trying to figure out how to conduct DD. I conduct my DD by checking out the companies website, products, locations, ceo history and board of directors, headquarters appeal (all because @daren put me on to this, it’s his thing 100%), to name a few things. I know there is more to it and it needs to be more technical so I asked chatGPT to come up with an editable template in order to conduct DD on potential companies I plan to invest in. If anyone has any edits or ideas I’m more than open to adjusting. Maybe something is left out? Maybe something doesn’t need to be there? I’m always open to suggestions ✌️

@FridgeFinancials has a great video on how to conduct DD

https://youtu.be/a7PUm8aduFk?si=TA6Mc2w1R_uYovbs

📊 Company Due Diligence (DD) Template

1. Basic Info
	•	Company Name & Ticker:
	•	Sector/Industry:
	•	Market Cap:
	•	Current Price / 52-Week Range:
	•	Headquarters & Operations Location(s):
	•	Primary Products/Services:


2. Investment Thesis (Why Consider This?)
	•	What problem are they solving?
	•	Unique value proposition / moat.
	•	Why now? (macro trends, sector growth, timing).

3. Business Model
	•	Revenue streams (product sales, subscriptions, licensing, etc.).
	•	Customers/clients (retail, enterprise, government).
	•	Geographic reach (domestic vs international).
	•	Partnerships or major contracts.

4. Financials

(use last 2–4 quarters or TTM)
	•	Revenue growth (YoY %):
	•	Profitability (Net Income, EPS):
	•	Margins (Gross, Operating):
	•	Cash Flow (positive/negative):
	•	Debt Load (Debt-to-Equity ratio):
	•	Cash on hand vs burn rate:

5. Valuation
	•	P/E ratio (if profitable):
	•	P/S ratio (if early stage/growth):
	•	EV/EBITDA or other key multiples vs sector averages.
	•	Compare valuation to competitors.

6. Growth Catalysts
	•	Sector tailwinds (AI, energy transition, biotech, etc.).
	•	Upcoming product launches or expansion.
	•	Government contracts or policy benefits.
	•	Strategic acquisitions/partnerships.

7. Risks
	•	Financial risks (cash burn, dilution risk, debt).
	•	Competitive risks (larger players, low barriers to entry).
	•	Regulatory risks (FDA, FCC, SEC, international laws).
	•	Market risks (commodity prices, interest rates, sector cycles).

8. Management & Ownership
	•	CEO & leadership track record.
	•	Insider ownership % (skin in the game).
	•	Institutional ownership % (hedge funds, ETFs).
	•	Recent insider buys/sells.

9. Momentum & Market Sentiment
	•	Analyst ratings (buy/hold/sell consensus).
	•	Short interest % (high = possible squeeze or bearish outlook).
	•	Recent news, hype, or controversy.
	•	Social media / retail investor sentiment.

10. Technical Analysis (Optional)
	•	Current trend (bullish, bearish, consolidation).
	•	Key support & resistance levels.
	•	Moving averages (50MA, 200MA).
	•	RSI/MACD signals.

11. Exit Strategy / Investment Horizon
	•	Target price range.
	•	Timeframe (short swing, mid-term catalyst, long-term hold).
	•	Stop-loss level (risk management).
	•	Position sizing plan (how much of portfolio).

12. Final Rating
	•	✅ Bullish Case Summary
	•	❌ Bearish Case Summary
	•	📈 Decision: Strong Buy / Buy / Neutral / Avoid / Sell
