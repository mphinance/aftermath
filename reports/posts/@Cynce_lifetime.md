### Post #28: 2025-06-13 14:14 `[Dd]`

**Friday 5: UVIX ain't got no job, ain't got shit to do, might as well get...**

Well, the past 18 hours have been interesting. I'm not going to break down the market for this because @CarlyBme is the best. I want to talk about market dynamics, risks, event velocity, and how they impact the volatility trades... IMO.

VIX is not an economic gauge. It's about sentiment. 

If everyone is happy and cool, VIX goes down into floating range. If people are worried, it grinds up. If people are complacent, it grinds down. If there is fear, hold on. If there is joy, put on a parachute.

Great, how does this impact UVIX. UVIX is a 2x levered mirror to the VIX but in Futures. This means it's not directly correlated and can converge or diverge more on sentiment. This is where velocity comes in too... for good and for bad.

Events come in different flavors. Everyday (economic and earnings news) predictable, surprise (bad earnings, tweets, geopolitical, macro), crisis (major impact that builds and is expected to stay). Each of these happen with a velocity. Everyday is often scheduled or a known risk, it causes a blip for intraday and goes away. Surprise,  like last night, hits with force, swings UVIX 5 to 25% rapidly, and then either retreats or escalates to crisis. Crisis can be fast onset and high velocity (tariffs) but also typically will have staying power that doesn't require same day action.

Don't chase any of this. If you are reactive, it's already over and you will be liquidity for someone else. 

UVIX is not a great options play. We don't often have great liquidity so it's OK for CCs but caution on anything else. And caution for CCs because events happen when they happen and you can miss massive upside because your holdings are locked into trades.

So how about a real example.

This week I had (have) 6201 shares at 25.70 cost basis. Great. I decided to cover most since everything was choppy and I was able to get great premiums ATM/ITM one strike above cost. Small pop and everything goes away with premiums and profit for next week and a reload. Big pop and you miss out on upside.

Last night had a high velocity event in Iran. I had 200 shares called away and left with 6000. All locked in. Had i left them uncovered i could have sold overnight at 31.59 and made 30k or something. But velocity goes both ways. 31.59 became low 28s this morning because retail is shaken out and IV subsides, futures stablize, and UVIX retreats. All this to say this trade takes commitment and active management plus a strategy on income vs gains vs both. All of this is impacted by velocity.

What happens, when it happens, and how quickly it's absorbed, all impact the profitability of this volatility trade. 

Anyway... I'm still going for those that have been following. I'll probably move up to 7000 to 7500 shares next dip with a goal of a 10k rotational position by end of summer. Follow if you like boring, niche trading like this...

**New VIX based levered ETFs - Roundhill S&P VIX Futures Points!**

Well @CarlyBme and @mphinance my SEC time was fruitful and I come bearing fruit for my volatility lovng friends. 

Quick summary 

Roundhill’s new suite of ETFs are built to track the S&P 500 VIX Short-Term Futures Points-Change Daily Index. This is a new approach to volatility that measures daily point changes in VIX futures rather than percentage moves. This structure attempts to offer more intuitive exposure for volatility traders who think in terms of absolute point swings rather than compounding returns. These ETFs don’t rebalance holdings to meet fixed leverage targets. They aim to mirror or counter the index’s daily movement, using front-month VIX futures contracts. There’s no dividend yield, returns are fully tied to the fund’s NAV movement, which tracks the point change in the VIX futures index before fees and expenses.

The lineup includes:

Roundhill Long VIX Futures Points ETF – basic long daily VIX futures exposure
Roundhill 2x Long VIX Futures Points ETF – leveraged 2× long version, rebalanced daily
Roundhill Short VIX Futures Points ETF – inverse daily exposure for downside volatility bets
Roundhill 2x Short VIX Futures Points ETF – aggressive 2× inverse for quick trades

These are not buy-and-hold, they’re built for tracking short-term moves in volatility. Like all leveraged and inverse products, compounding and decay could be a problem, but not if you are using them to swing.
