### 2026-09-07 15:27 — `$APP` `[Discuss]`

**When should you buy to close an option?**

Time to learn ya something while the market is closed.

The usual rule of thumb is to consider closing when you’ve captured more than 50% of the premium before 50% of the time has passed. But what happens when you capture a smaller profit extremely quickly? 
I recently sold three $APP $300 cash-secured puts with 293 days until expiration for $62 per share, collecting $18,600 in premium. 
The next day, $APP jumped roughly 10%, and I bought the puts back for $55. That locked in a $2,100 profit after holding the trade for only one day.

I captured just 11.29% of the available premium, but only 0.34% of the original time had elapsed.

That led me to create the Trade Efficiency Ratio:
* η = % of premium captured ÷ % of time elapsed

For this trade, η was 33.2. I captured premium roughly 33 times faster than time was passing... Which is pretty dang efficient despite only being 11% of the premium realized.

I wrote a full article explaining how I’m using this ratio to help decide when to buy options back early. Link in the comments.

I am looking at J.M. Smucker, ticker $SJM. Yes, the peanut butter and jelly company. They also have pet food, coffee, and a bunch of other packaged food brands, but most people know them for PB&J. What makes this interesting is that SJM is a pretty low-beta name, and the chart has confirmed breakout from a 3 year descending channel.

If you zoom way out to around 2000, the stock spent a long time inside a major ascending channel, increasing almost 2000%. The bottom of the channel acted as support multiple times, and the top acted as resistance, nice and simple. That trend eventually broke down in 2023. Since then, SJM has been stuck in a descending channel for the last few years. Lower highs, lower lows, back and forth inside the channel. Nothing too exciting.

But now it looks like the stock has broken through the upper side of that descending channel. The really interesting part is that this breakout area also lines up with former resistance from the old long-term channel. So there is a little bit of technical confluence here:

*  Long-term ascending channel from around 2000
*  Breakdown in 2023
* Multi-year descending channel after that
*  Recent breakout above the descending channel
*  Breakout overlaps with an old resistance area

The first level I would watch is around $140. That seems like the obvious resistance area based on most recent (3 years ago) prior highs. But my bigger target would be closer to $150. That is partly a psychological level, but it also lines up pretty well if you use the recent breakout move as a rough measured move.

The bullish thesis is simple: if SJM holds this breakout, it could have room to run toward $140 and maybe $150. The invalidation is also simple: if it falls back into the descending channel, the breakout probably failed. Ideally, we hold this breakout thru the end of the month to have stronger confirmation
